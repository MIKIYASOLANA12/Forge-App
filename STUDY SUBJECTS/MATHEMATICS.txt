# PHASE 2 — RECOMMENDED LEARNING ORDER

I am **not replacing the official syllabus order**. This is a study order designed around prerequisites and conceptual dependencies.

## Stage A — Core foundations

### 1. Grade 9 Further on Sets

Sets provide useful mathematical language for membership, collections, relationships, and later functions.

### 2. Grade 9 Number System

This should become extremely secure.

**Dependency chain:**

Number types
→ operations
→ real numbers
→ absolute value/interval ideas
→ later algebra

### 3. Grade 9 Solving Equations

This is one of the central algebra foundations.

**Dependency chain:**

linear equations
→ systems
→ nonlinear equations
→ later polynomial/exponential/logarithmic work

### 4. Grade 9 Solving Inequalities

Learn this immediately after equations.

**Dependency chain:**

equations
→ inequalities
→ systems of inequalities

→ quadratic inequalities
→ later linear programming

# COMMON MISUNDERSTANDING ZONES

These are places where our lessons will deliberately slow down.

### Algebra

* Rational vs irrational numbers
* Signs with negative numbers
* Equality vs inequality
* Reversing an inequality when multiplying/dividing by a negative number
* Extraneous solutions
* Treating an equation and an inequality as if they behave identically

### Functions

* Relation vs function
* Domain vs range
* Function notation
* Inverse function vs reciprocal
* Composition vs multiplication
* Graph interpretation

### Polynomial / Exponential / Logarithmic

* Zero/root vs coefficient
* Factor vs zero
* Exponent laws
* Logarithm as an exponent
* Restrictions on logarithmic expressions
* Confusing exponential growth with logarithmic behavior

### Trigonometry

* Opposite vs adjacent
* Which angle is being used
* Degrees vs radians
* Choosing the correct trigonometric ratio
* Identity vs equation

### Geometry

* Congruent vs similar
* Length ratio vs area ratio
* Interior vs exterior angle
* Arc vs sector vs segment
* Radius vs diameter

### Vectors / Matrices

* Scalar vs vector
* Direction vs magnitude
* Vector addition
* Matrix dimensions
* Matrix multiplication
* Determinant vs matrix
* Matrix inverse vs reciprocal

### Statistics / Probability

* Mean, median and mode
* Population vs sample
* Data representation
* Counting vs probability
* Permutation vs combination
* Event vs outcome

### Calculus

* Derivative vs derivative value
* Rate of change vs ordinary algebraic value
* Derivative vs integral
* Interpreting what a calculated result actually means

### Linear Programming

* Inequality direction
* Boundary lines
* Feasible region
* Maximum vs minimum
* Translating a real situation into mathematical constraints

# HOW I WILL TREAT THE OFFICIAL PDF

The books themselves establish the lesson structure around **activity → definition/theorem/note → examples → exercises**, and they explicitly emphasize practicing problems rather than merely reading explanations. 

For our course I will therefore preserve three things:

**Official syllabus:** exactly what appears in these books.

**Teaching explanation:** simpler explanations, analogies, derivations, examples, and connections that help you understand the official material.

**Outside information:** only when genuinely useful, and explicitly labeled:

> **Outside the provided PDF — optional explanation.**

I will also treat the numbered section headings as the official topic structure. For example, Grade 10's contents explicitly includes the full progression from **3.1 Exponents and Logarithms through 3.6 Applications**. 

---

# OUR MASTERY SYSTEM

We will **not** race through 29 blocks.

For each major topic, we will move through:

**Understand → Explain → Derive → Solve → Apply → Recognize tricks → Recall later → Re-test**

And I will keep bringing older material back into new lessons.

For example, after learning functions, you may later get an unfamiliar question that secretly tests domain and range. After inequalities, a later linear-programming problem may test the same inequality reasoning in a completely different setting.

That is intentional.
