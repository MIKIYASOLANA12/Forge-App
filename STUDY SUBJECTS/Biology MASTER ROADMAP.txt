# Grade 9–12 Biology MASTER ROADMAP

I’ve completed the roadmap stage before teaching anything. The four Ethiopian Ministry of Education Biology textbooks are the basis of the official scope: Grade 9, Grade 10, Grade 11, and Grade 12.    

I am treating the **numbered units, sections, and subsections in the textbooks as the syllabus map**. Activities, examples, review questions, and illustrations are learning material inside those topics rather than separate syllabus units.

One important source-handling rule: several contents pages contain numbering/encoding problems. I will **not silently invent missing titles or pretend the textbook numbering is cleaner than it is**.

# H. HIGH-RISK MISUNDERSTANDING AREAS

These are the areas where our lessons will deliberately slow down:

**Hypothesis vs conclusion**

**Theory vs hypothesis**

**Taxonomy vs nomenclature**

**Prokaryotic vs eukaryotic cells**

**Plant cell vs animal cell**

**Diffusion vs osmosis vs active transport**

**Mitosis vs meiosis**

**Asexual vs sexual reproduction**

**Photosynthesis vs cellular respiration**

**Enzyme vs substrate**

**Gene vs allele vs genotype vs phenotype**

**Dominant vs common**

**Mendelian vs non-Mendelian inheritance**

**Arteries vs veins**

**Hormone vs neurotransmitter**

**CNS vs PNS**

**Population vs community vs ecosystem**

**Food chain vs food web**

**Energy flow vs nutrient cycling**

**Weather/climate vs climate change**

**Natural selection vs an organism deliberately "choosing" to adapt**

**Evolution of populations vs changes in individual organisms**

**Virus vs cellular microorganism**

These will be treated as understanding problems, not merely definitions.

We now have the complete map.

We will **not** study Biology as:

> read page → memorize definition → answer question → forget.

We will study it as:

> **Foundation → mechanism → WHY → HOW → connection → example → exam wording → tricky application → active recall → spaced review → mastery**

And we will move **one topic at a time**, exactly as you specified.

Use a mixture of:

1. Normal question
2. Understanding question
3. WHY question
4. HOW question
5. Application question
6. Multiple choice
7. True/False
8. Matching
9. Short answer
10. Fill in the blank
11. Scenario-based question
12. Trick question
13. Exam-style question

Do not make every question multiple choice.

==================================================
7. MAKE ME THINK
==================================================

For important topics, ask me to explain things in my own words.

Example:

"Explain why mitochondria are important without using the textbook definition."

Then evaluate my understanding.

If my answer is weak:

Do NOT simply say:

❌ Wrong

Instead explain:

What I understood correctly
What I misunderstood
What concept I am missing
A simpler explanation
A new example
A retry question

9. RECORD EVERY QUESTION
==================================================

Every question must be permanently recorded.

Store:

- subject
- unit
- topic
- subtopic
- question
- question type
- difficulty
- correct answer
- user's answer
- correct/incorrect
- explanation
- timestamp
- attempt number
- response time if available

DO NOT lose question history.

==================================================
10. REPEATED MEMORY QUESTIONS
==================================================

IMPORTANT:

When I finish a topic, that topic is NOT forgotten.

Bring important concepts back later.

Ask the same concept using different wording.

Example:

Question 1:
What is the function of mitochondria?

Later:

Why does a cell need mitochondria?

Later:

A cell has increased energy demands. Which organelle becomes especially important and why?

Later:

Which statement about mitochondria is correct?

Later:

Explain mitochondria to a 10-year-old.

These may test the SAME underlying concept.

The system should track whether I genuinely understand it.

Do NOT simply repeat the identical question every time.

==================================================
11. WRONG ANSWER → WEAK AREA
==================================================

Whenever I answer incorrectly:

Record the mistake.

Example:

Topic:
Photosynthesis

Weak concept:
Role of chlorophyll

Accuracy:
40%

Then show:

⚠️ WEAK AREA

Photosynthesis — Chlorophyll

Recommended action:
Review chlorophyll's role and answer 5 targeted questions.

==================================================
12. TARGETED RETEST
==================================================

If I repeatedly miss a concept:

Generate another small practice set specifically targeting that concept.

Example:

3 wrong answers
→ targeted explanation
→ 5 targeted questions

If I improve:
→ return gradually to mixed questions.

If I continue failing:
→ teach the concept again using a different analogy/explanation.

==================================================
13. MASTERY
==================================================

Do NOT mark a Biology topic mastered because I checked a Todo box.

Todo completion means:

"I studied this."

Question performance determines:

"I understand this."

Use mastery states:

NOT_STARTED
EXPOSED
LEARNING
UNDERSTOOD
PRACTICING
STRONG
MASTERED

Mastery should be based on repeated performance across different question forms, not one lucky answer.

==================================================
14. UNIT COMPLETION
==================================================

When I finish all Biology topics in a unit:

Include:

- normal questions
- conceptual questions
- application questions
- tricky questions
- exam-style questions

Do not only test the final topic.

Include earlier topics from the unit too.
